How this calculator works

Each loan payment is split between two things: interest on the money you still owe, and principal, which is the part that actually reduces your balance. Early in a loan, most of each payment goes toward interest because the balance is still high. As the balance shrinks, more of each fixed payment goes toward principal instead — this shift is what the balance chart above shows.

This calculator uses the standard amortization formula banks use to set a fixed monthly payment, then walks through the loan month by month to total up exactly how much interest you'll pay and when you'll be done. Adding an extra monthly payment reduces the principal faster, which lowers the interest charged on every payment that follows.

Five ways to pay off a loan faster

  • ✓Add a fixed extra amount each month. Even $50–$200 extra, applied consistently, can cut years off a mortgage.
  • ✓Switch to biweekly payments. Paying half your monthly amount every two weeks results in one extra full payment per year.
  • ✓Put windfalls toward principal. Tax refunds, bonuses, and gifts do more good reducing your balance than sitting in a low-yield account.
  • ✓Round up every payment. Rounding a $1,798 payment up to $1,850 barely changes your budget but adds up over years.
  • ✓Refinance if rates drop meaningfully. A lower rate can free up cash to redirect as extra principal — model both scenarios above before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Does this work for mortgages, auto loans, and personal loans?

Yes. The math behind a fixed-rate loan is the same regardless of what it's for — only the typical amount, rate, and term differ. Just enter your own numbers.

Does paying extra each month actually make a difference?

Yes, and often more than people expect, because extra principal payments reduce the balance that future interest is calculated on. Try adding an extra payment above and compare the two lines on the balance chart.

Why does so much of my early payment go to interest?

Interest is calculated on your current balance, which is highest at the start of the loan. As you pay it down, the interest portion of each fixed payment shrinks and the principal portion grows.

Is this calculator accurate?

It uses the same fixed-rate amortization formula lenders use. It doesn't include taxes, insurance, PMI, or lender fees, so treat the results as a close estimate rather than an official loan quote.
